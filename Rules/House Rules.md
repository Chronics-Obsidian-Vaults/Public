# House rules

Campaign-specific rulings that override or extend the base 5e rules. Where a topic is not covered here, the standard rules apply.

## Spell preparation: prepared casters know their full list

For every class that normally *prepares* spells from a list (Cleric, Druid, Paladin, Wizard, Artificer, and any subclass that inherits this mechanic), the preparation step is dropped. The character is treated as **knowing** every spell on their class list that they could otherwise prepare, and may cast any of those spells using the appropriate spell slots.

The rule exists because preparation limits a caster's agency. Choosing a daily list forces a player to guess, before play begins, which spells the coming day will call for, and a wrong guess is punished for the whole adventuring day regardless of how the situation actually develops. Removing that step gives caster players, and caster NPCs alike, a broader range and the freedom to answer problems as they arise rather than as they were anticipated, which is the reason to play a prepared caster in the first place. The rule deliberately stops at prepared casters: for the classes that instead *know* a fixed set of spells, that narrow and permanent list is the defining constraint of the class, and it is left intact.

Practical effects:

- **No daily preparation.** There is no list of "prepared" spells and no need to swap them on a long rest.
- **Full class list is available.** Any spell of a level for which the character has slots, drawn from the class list, may be cast at will (subject to slots, components, and any other normal restrictions).
- **Spellbooks and prayer books become reference, not gatekeeping.** A wizard's spellbook and a cleric's or druid's holy focus are still narrative fixtures, but they do not restrict which spells the character can cast.
- **Always-prepared and domain/circle/oath spells** remain a feature of subclasses (they are simply also on the class list for the character).
- **Slots, components, and casting time are unchanged.** The house rule affects *access* to spells, not the economy of casting them.

Spontaneous casters (Sorcerer, Warlock, Bard, Ranger, and the *Spells Known* progression generally) are unaffected; they continue to know a fixed number of spells as printed.

In profiles and statblocks this rule is expressed as `Full [Class] list (homebrew, no prep)` in the spellcasting block, rather than enumerating individual prepared spells.
